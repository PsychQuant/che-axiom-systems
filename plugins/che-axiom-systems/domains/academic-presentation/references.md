# References — academic-presentation domain

External sources that ground and corroborate the axioms. The domain was first
distilled from the IMPS 2026 slide build and rehearsal feedback (2026-07-18);
these references show the same principles in published presentation guidance.

## R1. Chambers (2014), APA gradPSYCH

> Chambers, R. (2014). *Presenting your research effectively.* gradPSYCH
> Magazine, p. 28. American Psychological Association.
> https://www.apa.org/gradpsych/2014/09/presenting-research (archived)

APA's own graduate-student guidance on giving a research talk. It corroborates
several axioms and adds a few design specifics.

### Mapping to axioms

| Chambers (2014) says | Axiom corroborated |
|----------------------|--------------------|
| "consider your audience... scientists familiar with your area, or people who may never have had a class in psychology... structure with the appropriate depth and terminology" | **A7** Cross-Audience Term Grounding |
| "you want to tell a story, not lecture... the oral presentation as a whole should be the work of art, and the slides should be supplementary" | **A1** Example-First Grounding (story before slides) |
| "less is more... more slides with less content on each will help keep your audience focused on what you are saying and prevent them from staring blankly at your slides" | **A2** Single Channel per Moment |
| the billboard "three-seconds rule"; "if it takes more than three seconds to read the slide, consider revising it" | **A2** / **A4** (readable at a glance, not dense prose) |
| "a picture, chart or diagram can augment the text... audience has a potential preference for visual information" | **A6** Figure over Dense Table |
| "bar graphs should usually be your first choice, scatter plots a close second because they are simple... if the graph will look cleaner as two graphs instead of one, use two" | **A6** Figure over Dense Table |
| "accuracy of a graph is important... a restricted y-axis can make differences look much larger than they actually are... be ethical and ensure information is not misrepresented" | **A8** Honest Result Framing |
| "emphasize the main points so that your audience has a clear understanding of your take-home messages"; "ten minutes is not much time" | **A9** Time-Budget Discipline (take-aways within the slot) |

### Additional design specifics (not yet axiomatized)

Corroborating but finer-grained than the current axiom set; candidates for
future extension if they recur:

- **Colour palette** — ~3 complementary colours; be aware of colour affect
  (blue calming, red aggressive).
- **Animation** — usually not worth it; distracting; use only to add emphasis
  where value is clear.
- **Font / readability** — high contrast, easy-to-read font; font "personality"
  matters (Comic Sans inappropriate for a conference).

## R2. Cheng et al. (2023), IMPS oral talk — user's own exemplar

> Cheng, C., Yang, H.-H., & Hsu, Y.-F. (2023). *Identifiability of polychoric
> models with latent elliptical distributions* [Oral presentation].
> International Meeting of the Psychometric Society (IMPS), July 27.
> Deck: `July 27-900a-Cheng.pptx` (32 slides + 32 speaker-notes pages).

The author's own prior conference talk, kept as a first-person exemplar of the
axioms in practice. Unlike R1 (external guidance), this is the source-of-truth
for what the author actually does when the axioms are followed unselfconsciously.

### What the deck demonstrates

| Observed in the deck | Axiom / theorem instantiated |
|----------------------|------------------------------|
| Title → **Outline slide** (slide 2), then concepts before questions | **A5** Navigational Scaffolding |
| Plain noun-phrase titles: "Outline", "Identifiability Constraints", "Research Questions", "General Discussion", "Conclusions", "Future directions" | **A4** Plain Register |
| Slide 3 opens with a concrete claim + figure (7-point Likert as categorization of a normal variable) before the abstract identifiability machinery | **A1** Example-First Grounding |
| "Identifiability" defined in place using multiple linear regression as the familiar example; "Definition of Polychoric Models" stated before use | **A7** Cross-Audience Term Grounding |
| **"Research Questions" slide recurs at 14/15/18/20/24**, each return revealing the next answer | **T3** Recurring Anchor Slide |
| Every one of the 32 slides carries a **complete spoken script in the speaker-notes layer** while the slide face stays sparse | **T2** Verbatim-Slide Crutch (notes-layer form — the general solution) |

The notes-layer scripting is the important correction to any reading of T2 as
"put verbatim on the slide": the author's established practice is a sparse slide
face plus a full script in the notes, read from the confidence monitor. On-slide
verbatim is the fallback for when no confidence monitor is available.

### One noted divergence — bullet points

Chambers advises **against** bullet points: *"Sometimes you may feel a need to
use bullet points. Do not. Typesetting can replace bullet points... consider
bolding and increasing the font size of parent lines and indenting child
lines."*

This diverges from the working practice adopted in the IMPS build, where the
user asked for **formal bullet lists** ("正式列點") for the format-description
slides. Both are defensible: Chambers optimises for a billboard-clean look;
formal bullets optimise for a scannable, parallel structure the speaker can read
under A2/T2 (verbatim-slide crutch). Recorded as a genuine trade-off, not
silently resolved — the choice depends on whether the slide is a
few-words billboard or a read-along script surface.
